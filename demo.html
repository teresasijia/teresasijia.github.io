What do the models think of you?

Answer a few bets. Then four theories of ambiguity predict a kind of bet you were never asked about.

There is a box of 100 balls, each green, blue, yellow or orange. You are not told how many of each. Five balls were drawn, with replacement, and here is what came out:

Your box and your draws are sampled fresh. Reload for a different one.

Now you will be offered a series of bets. Each time, you choose between betting on a colour from that box — where you do not know the odds — and a bet that pays with a stated chance, like a coin or a spinner.

There are no right answers, and the questions are not fixed in advance. Each one is chosen by an adaptive procedure that picks whichever question it expects to learn the most from, given everything you have said so far. That is the method the research uses; this is a short version of it.

Answer with your gut. The procedure adapts either way.

Each column is a different theory of how people bet when they do not know the odds, fitted to your answers. The numbers are matching probabilities: the known chance at which that model says you would be indifferent.

The same thing as a curve

Read it as: for an event of any given probability, what known chance would make you indifferent? The dashed line is what someone indifferent to ambiguity would do. Distance from it is your fitted ambiguity attitude.

And here is the open question

Those models mostly agree with each other, because you gave them one box, and any of them can be bent to fit one box. They stop agreeing when you change the box — when the odds get sharper or vaguer than the ones you were measured on. Some of these models carry your attitude across that change; at least one of them cannot see the change at all.

Which of them travels is an empirical question, and it is what my job market paper is about. Read about it here.

Method. Questions are chosen by maximising expected information gain under a neo-additive model with a free belief; the models are then re-fitted to the same answers on a finer grid, with belief restricted to a one-parameter update (Aydogan 2021) except for the smooth model, which assumes Bayesian updating. The single- and triple-colour bets are what the procedure may ask; the two-colour bets are held out by construction, exactly as in the experiment this is drawn from. Nothing is stored and nothing is sent anywhere — it all runs in your browser.
